Special Education Schools

School placement runs on a separate track from OPWDD, through the NYC Department of Education's Committee on Special Education (CSE) and the IEP process; the two often intersect, especially around evaluations and Medicaid. This section covers public, state-funded/approved, and private school options.

Step 1

Getting an evaluation & an IEP (Committee on Special Education)

Any NYC child can be referred to their school's Committee on Special Education (CSE) for a free evaluation to determine whether they qualify for an Individualized Education Program (IEP) and specialized school placement.

  • NYSED: The CSE/CPSE Process & IEP Development Official · NY State

    The state's overview of how the Committee on Special Education process and IEP development work.

  • NYC DOE: Committee on Special Education (CSE) Official · NYC DOE

    DOE's own explanation of what a CSE is and how it's assigned.

  • NYC DOE: Requesting a Special Education Evaluation Official · NYC DOE

    Step-by-step instructions for formally requesting an evaluation in writing.

  • NYC DOE: The IEP Process: Evaluation Official · NYC DOE

    Where evaluation fits into the overall IEP timeline.

  • NYC DOE: Special Education Standard Operating Procedures Manual (SOPM) Official · NYC DOE · Reference manual

    DOE's own internal procedures manual for special education: the detailed rulebook that lays out exactly what schools and CSEs are required to do, and by when, at every stage: referral, evaluation timelines, IEP development and annual review, reevaluation, related services, and placement. It's written for DOE staff, not families, so it's dense; but because it documents the DOE's own required procedures, it's a useful reference if a school seems to be skipping steps or missing timelines, including in a reimbursement or impartial hearing case (see Evaluations, Sub-category 4).

  • INCLUDEnyc Free · Nonprofit

    Free, one-on-one help center support for navigating CSE evaluations, IEPs, and school placement disputes.

Step 2

DOE public school special education programs

Most NYC students with IEPs are served in specialized public-school settings: free, since they're part of the DOE system.

  • NYC DOE: Specialized Programs for Students with Disabilities Official · NYC DOE · Free

    Overview of DOE special-education school settings, including District 75 programs for students with more intensive needs.

  • NYC Autism Charter School Free · Public charter · Lottery

    A tuition-free public charter school specifically for students with autism, in the Bronx and East Harlem. Admission is by application/lottery, not a CSE placement; see their Apply page.

  • NYC DOE: Autism (ASD) Family Resource Guide Official · NYC DOE · Free

    Explains the two DOE autism-specific program models: ASD Nest (integrated co-teaching classes, partnered with NYU, for students with average-to-above-average academic skills) and ASD Horizon (small self-contained classes, max. 8 students, for students needing more support). Per this guide, the DOE does not publish a current site list and does not accept requests for a specific school: "student applications are assigned a specific school." See also the NYLPI Nest & Horizon factsheet for a plain-language comparison, and ask your CSE how to apply for either program.

  • Current program size & how to get your child's actual site Citywide totals · Jan 2025

    Neither program publishes a school-by-school site list, but DOE gave the Council these citywide figures on January 30, 2025: ASD Nest: 88 schools, 442 classes, 1,972 students, grades K–12; ASD Horizon: 69 schools, 255 classes, 1,853 students, grades K–12. Both programs have also expanded in recent years (e.g., 160 new seats added in January 2024, concentrated in Districts 5, 12, and 14) so the site mix keeps shifting. To find out which specific school your own child would be assigned to, email ASDPrograms@schools.nyc.gov or ask your CSE directly: DOE will only confirm a site in the context of an actual application.

  • Historical Horizon Program site list (unofficial, parent-compiled) Unverified · Historical (~2019–2022) · Not an official DOE source

    A list of schools that hosted ASD Horizon classes, compiled by a parent from a citywide DOE budget document (2019–2020 class counts) and school-by-school notes gathered through roughly 2022. This is not the current DOE site list: the DOE does not publish one, sites and class counts change from year to year, and a school on this list may no longer host the program (or may host it under a different configuration) today. Use it only as a starting point for questions to ask your CSE or a school directly, never as confirmation that a program currently exists at a given site.

Step 3

State-funded / approved schools ("853" & "4410" schools)

When a public-school setting isn't appropriate, a CSE can place a student at a State-approved nonpublic school; often called "853 schools" (school-age) or "4410 schools" (preschool). These are privately run but publicly funded, so families generally pay no tuition once the CSE places the child there.

  • NYSED: Approved Private, Special Act, State-Operated & State-Supported Schools Official · NY State

    The official overview of these state-funded school categories.

  • NYSED: Approved Special Education Programs (Preschool & School-Age) Official · NY State

    Landing page for both the 4410 (preschool) and school-age approved-program lists below.

  • NYSED: 4410 Approved Preschool Programs list (spreadsheet) Official · NY State

    The current statewide list of approved preschool special-education programs.

  • NYSED: Approved Private (853) Schools list (spreadsheet) Official · NY State

Evaluations

A formal psychological, psychosocial, or neuropsychological evaluation gets used for more than one purpose, and it's worth knowing that up front: the same kind of report can support an OPWDD eligibility application (see OPWDD Application & Enrollment, Step 2 and Step 3), a special-education school enrollment or CSE placement decision (see Special Education Schools, Step 1), and a DOE reimbursement or impartial hearing case if the DOE's recommended placement is being challenged. Below are five sub-categories: free no-Medicaid evaluators, general Article 16 clinics, private neuropsych/clinical providers, DOE reimbursement/impartial-hearing resources, and the DOE's CPSE evaluator list for ages 3–5. Call ahead in every case to confirm current wait time, cost, and what's accepted.

Sub-category 1 · Start here

Free evaluations: no active Medicaid required

These two organizations have a special New York State grant specifically to evaluate individuals for OPWDD eligibility who do not have active Medicaid: no cost, and in Eden II's case, no waiting list as of this writing. Confirm current openings directly, since grant-funded capacity can change.

  • Eden II Programs: Clinical Evaluation Program Free · No active Medicaid · No waitlist

    Staten Island and Brooklyn locations, ages 3+, in-person or virtual. Psychological, psychosocial, and autism testing for OPWDD eligibility. Per Eden II's own flyer: individuals can NOT have active Medicaid to use this grant-funded evaluation. Email fssevaluations@eden2.org to schedule.

  • Sinergia, Inc.: Family Support / Evaluations Free · No active Medicaid

    Also accepts non-Medicaid families for OPWDD evaluation under the same type of NYS grant funding. Contact Sinergia's Family Support team directly to confirm current openings and scheduling.

Sub-category 3

Most bill through private insurance or self-pay; call ahead to confirm current rates and which insurance plans they accept.

Step 0

Start here: what is OPWDD, and am I eligible?

OPWDD determines eligibility based on a documented developmental disability that began before age 22 and is expected to be lifelong. These resources explain the criteria and the overall process before you make first contact.

  • OPWDD: Eligibility overview Official · NY State

    The state's own explanation of who qualifies for OPWDD services.

  • Eligibility for OPWDD Services: Important Facts (Feb 2026 PDF) Official · NY State

    A short, plain-language fact sheet on eligibility criteria and documentation needed.

  • Advocates for Children: "Applying for OPWDD" guide Free · Nonprofit

    A parent-facing step-by-step guide to the application process, written by a legal advocacy organization for families. Confirm details are current, as some specifics may lag official updates.

  • INCLUDEnyc: OPWDD help center article Free · Nonprofit

    A NYC-focused nonprofit's explainer on what OPWDD is and how the pieces fit together.

  • OPWDD: Determining Eligibility for Services (detailed guidelines PDF) Official · NY State

    The full, detailed eligibility standard. New York's Mental Hygiene Law requires all four of: (1) a qualifying developmental disability, (2) onset before age 22, (3) expected to continue indefinitely, and (4) a substantial handicap to functioning. The documentation package OPWDD asks for to determine eligibility typically includes: a psychological report within 3 years including an IQ assessment (find a provider via OPWDD's assessment-provider finder); an adaptive-behavior assessment (interpretive report with summary/composite/domain scores if IQ is above 60, or one based on caregiver interviews, records, and observation if IQ is below 60; an Early Intervention Multidisciplinary Core Evaluation can work for young children if it includes standardized cognitive/language/adaptive/social/motor scores); a general medical report from within the past 365 days; a social/developmental history or psychosocial report showing disability onset before age 22; and a social evaluation from within the past 365 days prepared by a Care Manager/supervisor, QIDP, LMSW/LCSW, psychologist, or someone with a Master's in psychology who knows the individual.

Step 3

Apply for Medicaid & the HCBS Waiver

Most ongoing OPWDD services (Care Management, Self-Direction, Habilitation, respite, and more) require Medicaid and enrollment in the HCBS (Home and Community-Based Services) Waiver. Applying for Medicaid itself has no application fee.

  • OPWDD: About Medicaid and OPWDD services Official · NY State

    How Medicaid connects to OPWDD services and why most families need it.

  • OPWDD: 23-ADM-06R: Individual Eligibility & Enrollment for the HCBS Waiver Official · NY State

    The formal administrative guidance on waiver eligibility and requesting service authorization; detailed, but the authoritative source.

  • NY Health Access: Medicaid 1915(c) HCBS Waiver Programs in New York State Free · Nonprofit

    A consumer-health legal-aid site's plainer explanation of how the HCBS Waiver works alongside Medicaid.

  • Ways to apply for Medicaid Practical · Multiple options

    If you're not already enrolled, you can apply: directly through your county Medicaid office; through NY State of Health at 1-855-355-5777; through a community services agency you already work with (many can help complete the application and explain required documents); or once OPWDD's Front Door refers you to a Care Coordination Organization, which will assist with Medicaid enrollment. General application help is also available at 1-800-541-2831.

  • Medicaid's special provisions for developmental disabilities Good to know

    A few Medicaid rules apply specifically to people with developmental disabilities: individuals can work and still qualify for Medicaid, though depending on income they may need to contribute to service costs; parental income and resources can be waived (not counted) for children living at home who are seeking enrollment in the OPWDD HCBS Waiver program; and financial resources can be placed in a Medicaid-qualifying supplemental needs trust and remain exempt for Medicaid purposes. Ask your Care Manager or a benefits advisor how these apply to your specific situation.

Step 5

Explore Self-Direction

Self-Direction lets families design and manage an individualized budget for supports and services, including Individual Directed Goods and Services (IDGS). This is optional and typically becomes available once a Life Plan is in place, but is worth understanding early.

Looking for fee-based classes, camps, and recreation programs, several of which accept Self-Direction/IDGS or Fiscal Intermediary reimbursement? See Community Activities & Providers: Sports, recreation & enrichment.

  • In The Driver's Seat: Individual Directed Goods and Services (IDGS) Free · Nonprofit

    Explains IDGS specifically: the budget category that funds many community classes and equipment.

  • INCLUDEnyc: Key terms for Self-Directed services Free · Nonprofit

    A glossary that helps decode Self-Direction terminology (Fiscal Intermediary, broker, budget, etc.).

  • OPWDD: Self-Direction for Providers Official · NY State

    The state's own Self-Direction resource hub (written for providers, but useful background for families too).

  • How Self-Direction is actually funded Good to know

    Self-Directed services are paid for out of a Personal Resource Account (PRA): an individualized budget amount based on the person's assessed needs. The PRA can cover things like respite, community habilitation, classes, memberships, and transportation, but it comes with real record-keeping and administrative work (tracking spending, paying a Fiscal Intermediary, documenting services). Plan for that time commitment, or ask your Care Manager about broker/support-broker help.
